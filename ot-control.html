Rotate forward, never backward

Nine hours of work in a twenty-four hour day leaves fifteen. Whether a man actually gets them depends on one thing: whether tomorrow's shift starts later than today's, or earlier.

FORWARD — A LATER START

16 – 21 hours

Opening today, closing tomorrow. He finishes at 21:00 and is not due back until 18:30. This is what we roster.

THE SAME SHIFT AGAIN

15 hours

The arithmetic floor of a nine-hour day. Acceptable, and what most men have now.

BACKWARD — AN EARLIER START

9 hours

Closing at 03:30, opening at 12:30. Never to be rostered again.

The cycle every man follows

Opening  → 16h →  Closing  → 33h →  Rest day  →  Opening again

The one backward step in the whole week is the one that follows the rest day, and that step has a full day inside it. The rest day always comes after the closing block, never before it.

Drivers: at least 4 hours between two runs, whatever the roster says.

Overtime: capped at 9 hours a day per branch — 270 a month.

Ardiya transport: leave early enough to be on site at 12:00.

Gap figures follow from the proposed band times and a nine-hour shift; they move with the final closing times. The rule itself does not: a later start always lengthens the rest, an earlier one always shortens it.

How the roster is actually built

The men are put into buddy pairs — two names that share a rest day and carry the same overtime. Four rules then decide the week, and between them they leave almost no freedom: for most branches there is one roster that works, not many.

ONE

One pair rests each day

Saturday to Wednesday only. Every man knows his day, and it is the same day every week.

TWO

Never an earlier start

So a man does his First-shift days, then moves to Second shift, and goes back only after a rest day.

THREE

The double is the changeover

One long day covering both shifts, worked on the day he crosses from First to Second. It is the only overtime in the week.

FOUR

A supervisor on each shift

Every day, both shifts. At Jabriya that needs a third man named as acting supervisor.

WHAT THE RULES RULED OUT

A five-hour stay-late cannot be rostered. A man who finishes at 02:30 cannot open at 12:30, so he is pushed onto Second shift and cannot come back that week. Four hours is the ceiling on a stay-late; beyond that it has to be a full double.

WHY BUDDIES SPLIT ACROSS SHIFTS

Whole pairs give four or six men on a shift, never five. So buddies share the rest day and the overtime but may stand on different shifts — which also means one of the pair is on site nearly all the time.

Rest between two shifts is never allowed below eleven hours, and on these rosters the shortest is twelve. Pairs are taken straight down the branch list; where a branch has two supervisors they are placed in different pairs. Every roster in this deck was generated against these rules and checked, not drawn by hand.
